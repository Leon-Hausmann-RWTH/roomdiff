# Design note

## Principle: change needs evidence

Both videos are reconstructed together, so every keyframe has depth and a pose in one metric frame. A surface the before video saw counts as gone only if frames of the after video looked *through* the place where it was: the after depth along those rays lies beyond the point (evidence of absence, as in Fehr et al., ICRA 2017, and Khronos, RSS 2024; the per-pixel test is SceneDiff's `foreground_distance`). A nearer after depth means something blocks the view, and a place no after frame reaches was never filmed; neither can produce a change. The same test runs from after to before for additions. The rest is counting these votes per point and per object, and reporting what stayed undecided instead of hiding it.

## Decisions

1. **One joint feed-forward reconstruction.** Splat-based detectors assume a dense before capture; on SceneDiff's casual pairs 3DGS-CD reaches 0.5 obj/sc AP against 22.8. One Pi3X pass needs no alignment step: on the report pairs a median of 0.2 % of the static background comes out free. Pi3 was not tried (both checkpoints did not fit on the disk); Pi3X was kept because this static-background check passed and its metric scale feeds the sentences and the thresholds.
2. **Ray evidence, not nearest-neighbour differencing.** Differencing calls every region one video did not cover a change: on the report pairs it reports 435 changes at precision 0.08, 183 of them where the other video never looked. The ray test reports 54 at precision 0.57 with 1 there, and none on six half-video controls of an unchanged room, where differencing reports 37.
3. **Votes from distinct camera positions.** Neighbouring keyframes see nearly the same ray, so one burst of bad depth looks like many votes. Requiring that cameras in two places looked through at least 30 % of a candidate removed 33 of 45 false candidates on the tuning pairs and kept 14 of 17 detectable objects. A thickness rule and a free-margin rule were tried and dropped: the first rejects objects seen from one side, the second did not separate true from false candidates.
4. **Verdicts with reasons and a place to look.** Each object is confirmed, unverified (hidden, out of view, or partly still there) or dismissed from the states of its points above the contact zone. Hidden or unseen objects come with the camera pose that would settle them. A confirmed removal without a partner triggers a search of the other video for the same object; on the report pairs this raised the share of found changes with the right type from 0.45 to 0.71.
5. **Models only name.** VLMs are poor change detectors (7.1 against 43.8 obj/im AP in SceneDiff's comparison), so none decides anything. SigLIP 2 names masked crops in-process, with no server. Supports are found in 3D and only named from an image; heights, distances, turns and blocked floor area come from the points.

## Results and failure modes

On 18 indoor SceneDiff test pairs, roomdiff finds 62 % of the 50 changed objects and 77 % of those 8 cm or taller, at precision 0.57; the official evaluator gives 42.4 px/im IoU, 27.6 obj/im AP and 15.7 obj/sc AP on this subset (SceneDiff: 39.1, 43.8 and 22.8 on its full set). A pair takes about 2 minutes on an M2 Max. Flat objects (under 8 cm) stay inside the depth band (27 % recall); a move whose new place the other video never saw stays a removal unless the search finds it; deformable objects split; labels can be confidently wrong. Next: an appearance cue on points that test as seen, for flat objects, and a loop that films again from the suggested viewpoints.
